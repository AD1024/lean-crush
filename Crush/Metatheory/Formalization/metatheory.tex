\begin{abstract}
This document specifies the proof-facing higher-order language used by Crush,
its first-order target, their model semantics, closure conversion by
defunctionalization, and the semantic soundness theorem.  It follows the Lean
development and deliberately distinguishes the verified mathematical core from
the refinement boundary to the live translator.  The displayed notation agrees
with the centralized Lean notation file.\leanfile{Notation.lean}
\end{abstract}

\tableofcontents

\section{Overview and notation}
\sectioncontents{The verified pipeline; conventions; correspondence with Lean notation.}

The source language is a simply typed higher-order logic with opaque base sorts.
The target is many-sorted first-order logic.  Defunctionalization replaces every
function space by an opaque first-order sort, every lambda by a closure
constructor, and every higher-order application by an application symbol.

\subsection*{Metavariable conventions}

The following conventions apply before any formulas below are read:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\quad}l}
 e,f,a & \text{terms (with $f$ usually function-valued and $a$ an argument)},\\
 \varphi,\psi & \text{Boolean formulas; $\varphi$ is closed when called a sentence},\\
 \beta & \text{an opaque source base sort},\\
 \sigma,\tau,\upsilon & \text{source types},\\
 \kappa & \text{a first-order target sort},\\
 \Gamma & \text{a local typing context},\\
 \Sigma & \text{a signature of uninterpreted constants or symbols},\\
 \mathcal M & \text{a semantic model},\\
 \rho & \text{a valuation assigning semantic values to local variables},\\
 \nu & \text{an individual semantic value},\\
 R & \text{a bundled relation between a source model and a target model},\\
 \Delta & \text{a target theory, that is, a list of closed formulas},\\
 \mathcal D & \text{the core syntax-to-syntax defunctionalization map},\\
 \mathcal T(\varphi) & \text{the complete target theory generated from $\varphi$}.
\end{array}
\]
Subscripts $s$ and $t$ mean \emph{source} and \emph{target}; for example,
$\mathcal M_s$ and $\mathcal M_t$ are source and target models, while $\rho_s$
and $\rho_t$ are their valuations.  A symbol without a subscript is used when
the side is clear or the statement applies to either language.  We use Greek
metavariables where conventional, rather than program-style identifiers.

\begin{definition}[Carrier]
A \emph{carrier} is the collection of semantic values that a model assigns to
a logical sort.  For example, if $\beta$ is an opaque sort intended to represent
entities, a model may choose some nonempty type $B(\beta)$ as its carrier; the
variables and constants of sort $\beta$ then denote elements of $B(\beta)$.
Syntax specifies only the name and relationships of sorts, whereas a model
chooses their carriers.  We require carriers to be nonempty, following the
standard many-sorted and SMT semantics.
\end{definition}

For a closed source formula $\varphi$, the central result has the shape
\[
  \operatorname{Unsat}_{\mathrm{FO}}\bigl(\mathcal T(\varphi)\bigr)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \operatorname{Unsat}_{\mathrm{HO}}(\varphi).
\]
This is the direction needed to justify reporting unsatisfiability after the
translation.

We use the following notation throughout:
\[
\begin{array}{c@{\qquad}l}
  \den{e}{\mathcal M}{\rho} & \text{denotation of $e$ in $\mathcal M$ under $\rho$},\\
  \mathcal M\sat\varphi & \text{satisfaction of a closed formula},\\
  \mathcal M\satt\Delta & \text{satisfaction of every sentence in a theory},\\
  \erase{\tau} & \text{first-order erasure of a source type},\\
  \df{e} & \text{core defunctionalization of syntax},\\
  \erase{\Gamma}^{\star} & \text{pointwise erasure of a context},\\
  \vrel{\nu_s}{R,\tau}{\nu_t} & \text{logical relation on values},\\
  \rrel{\rho_s}{R}{\rho_t} & \text{pointwise logical relation on valuations},\\
  \FV(e) & \text{duplicate-free free-variable positions of $e$}.
\end{array}
\]

For example, $\den{e}{\mathcal M}{\rho}$ should be read from the conventions
above as: ``the semantic value of term $e$, using the interpretations supplied
by model $\mathcal M$ and the local-variable assignment $\rho$.''  The model
interprets global constants and carriers; the valuation interprets only the
locally bound variables in $e$.

\section{Intrinsically typed higher-order source}
\sectioncontents{Types and signatures; typed references; terms and formulas.}

\subsection{Types, contexts, and signatures}

\begin{definition}[Source types]
Let $\beta$ range over opaque base-sort identifiers.  Source types are generated
by
\[
  \tau ::= \Bool \mid \Base(\beta) \mid \tau\to\tau.
\]
Arrow types are nondependent and associate to the right.  A context is a list
$\Gamma=[\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_{n-1}]$, with the most recently introduced binder at
the head.  A signature $\Sigma$ is likewise a list of the types of uninterpreted
constants.\leanfile{HO/Core.lean}
\end{definition}

Typed de Bruijn references are judgments $i:\tau\in\Gamma$, inductively
generated by
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=here]
    { }
    {0:\tau\in(\tau::\Gamma)}

  \inferrule*[right=there]
    {i:\tau\in\Gamma}
    {i+1:\tau\in(\sigma::\Gamma)}.
\end{mathpar}
The same representation indexes constants in $\Sigma$.  Consequently,
ill-typed variables and constants are not inhabitants of the syntax datatype.

\subsection{Terms}

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic source syntax]
The judgment $\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e:\tau$ is generated by the following forms:
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=var]
    {i:\tau\in\Gamma}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash x_i:\tau}

  \inferrule*[right=const]
    {k:\tau\in\Sigma}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash c_k:\tau}

  \inferrule*[right=bool]
    {b\in\{\top,\bot\}}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash b:\Bool}

  \inferrule*[right=not]
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\Bool}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\neg\varphi:\Bool}

  \inferrule*[right=connective]
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\Bool \\
     \Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\psi:\Bool \\
     \circ\in\{\land,\lor,\Rightarrow,\Leftrightarrow\}}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\varphi\circ\psi:\Bool}

  \inferrule*[right=eq]
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e_1:\tau \\
     \Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e_2:\tau}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e_1=e_2:\Bool}

  \inferrule*[right=lam]
    {\Sigma;\tau::\Gamma\vdash e:\sigma}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash\lambda x{:}\tau.e:\tau\to\sigma}

  \inferrule*[right=app]
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash f:\tau\to\sigma \\
     \Sigma;\Gamma\vdash a:\tau}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash f\,a:\sigma}

  \inferrule*[right=quant]
    {\Sigma;\tau::\Gamma\vdash\varphi:\Bool \\
     Q\in\{\forall,\exists\}}
    {\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash Qx{:}\tau.\varphi:\Bool}.
\end{mathpar}
A formula is a Boolean term, and a sentence is a formula in the empty context.
The indices of Lean's \texttt{Term} datatype enforce all premises of these
rules.\leanfile{HO/Core.lean}
\end{definition}

Notice that quantification may range over arrow types.  Thus ``higher-order''
describes both lambda/application and the possible domains of quantifiers.

\section{Higher-order model semantics}
\sectioncontents{Interpretation of types; models and valuations; denotation and satisfaction.}

\begin{definition}[Type denotation]
A family $B$ assigns a nonempty carrier $B(\beta)$, represented by a Lean type,
to each opaque base sort.  Its extension to all source types is
\[
  \llbracket\Bool\rrbracket_B=\mathsf{Prop},\qquad
  \llbracket\Base(\beta)\rrbracket_B=B(\beta),\qquad
  \llbracket\tau\to\sigma\rrbracket_B=
    \llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B\to\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket_B.
\]
\leanfile{HO/Semantics.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Source model and valuation]
A source model $\mathcal M_s$ consists of nonempty carriers $B(\beta)$ and, for
each $k:\tau\in\Sigma$, a value
$c_k^{\mathcal M_s}\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B$.  A valuation $\rho_s$ for
$\Gamma$ assigns to every typed reference $i:\tau\in\Gamma$ a value in
$\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B$.  We write $\rho_s[\nu]$ for extension by a new
head value $\nu$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Source denotation]
The function $\den{e}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}$ is defined structurally.  Variables
are looked up in $\rho_s$, constants in $\mathcal M_s$, Boolean constructs have
their ordinary propositional meanings, and equality is Lean equality.  The
distinctive clauses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \den{\lambda x{:}\tau.e}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
   &= \lambda\nu.\den{e}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s[\nu]},\\
 \den{f\,a}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
   &= \den{f}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
      (\den{a}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}),\\
 \den{\forall x{:}\tau.\varphi}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
   &= \forall\nu\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B,
      \den{\varphi}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s[\nu]},\\
 \den{\exists x{:}\tau.\varphi}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
   &= \exists\nu\in\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B,
      \den{\varphi}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s[\nu]}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For a sentence $\varphi$, $\mathcal M_s\sat\varphi$ abbreviates evaluation
under the empty valuation.\leanfile{HO/Semantics.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Nonemptiness]
If every $B(\beta)$ is nonempty, then $\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_B$ is nonempty
for every $\tau$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By induction on $\tau$.  At an arrow, choose a constant function returning an
inhabitant of the codomain.
\end{proof}

\section{Intrinsically typed first-order target}
\sectioncontents{First-order sorts and symbols; target syntax; target semantics.}

\subsection{Sorts and declarations}

\begin{definition}[First-order erasure]
Target sorts are
\[
  \kappa ::= \Bool\mid\Base(\beta)\mid\mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma).
\]
The erasure of a source type is
\[
 \erase{\Bool}=\Bool,\qquad
 \erase{\Base(\beta)}=\Base(\beta),\qquad
 \erase{\tau\to\sigma}=\mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma).
\]
The last form is an opaque first-order sort, not a function space.  Contexts are
erased pointwise: $\erase{[\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_n]}^{\star}
=[\erase{\tau_0},\ldots,\erase{\tau_n}]$.\leanfile{FO/Core.lean}
\end{definition}

A symbol declaration $\delta=(\bar\kappa;\kappa)$ consists of a list of argument
sorts and one result sort.  A target signature is a list of such declarations.
Typed symbol references and heterogeneous argument vectors ensure that a symbol
can only be applied at its declared arity and sorts.

\begin{definition}[Target syntax]
The first-order term judgment contains variables, symbol application, Boolean
literals and connectives, same-sort equality, and universal and existential
quantification.  It contains neither lambda nor higher-order application.
Function values occur only as elements of $\mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma)$.
\leanfile{FO/Core.lean}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Target semantics}

\begin{definition}[Target model]
Target carriers $C$ provide nonempty types $C_B(\beta)$ and
$C_F(\tau,\sigma)$.  Sort denotation maps $\mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma)$ to the
arbitrary carrier $C_F(\tau,\sigma)$, rather than to a meta-level function
space.  A declaration $(\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_n;\kappa)$ denotes the curried
type
\[
  \llbracket\kappa_1\rrbracket_C\to\cdots\to
  \llbracket\kappa_n\rrbracket_C\to\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket_C.
\]
A target model $\mathcal M_t$ chooses an interpretation of this type for every
symbol.  Term denotation, sentence satisfaction $\mathcal M_t\sat\varphi$, and
theory satisfaction $\mathcal M_t\satt\Delta$ are standard.\leanfile{FO/Semantics.lean}
\end{definition}

The actual core translation uses a type-indexed family of symbols rather than a
finite list while proving semantics.  Family syntax and semantics differ only in
how symbols are indexed; this avoids irrelevant finite-signature bookkeeping in
the fundamental proof.\leanfiles{FO/Family.lean and FO/FamilySemantics.lean}

\section{Collection and generated declarations}
\sectioncontents{Free variables; closures and arrows; total collection; generated symbols.}

\begin{definition}[Free-variable positions]
$\FV(e)$ is computed structurally as a left-to-right, duplicate-free list of
de Bruijn positions.  Under a binder, position $0$ is removed and every
$i+1$ becomes $i$.  Binary forms concatenate their lists and erase duplicates.
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/Collect.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure descriptor]
A closure descriptor is a tuple
\[
  \chi=(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e)
  \quad\text{where}\quad
  \Sigma;\tau::\Gamma\vdash e:\sigma.
\]
Its captures are $\mathsf{leave}(\FV(e))$, hence are positions in $\Gamma$, not
in $\tau::\Gamma$.  Total typed lookup converts these positions to an
existentially packed list of typed references; their types form
$\mathsf{captureTypes}(\chi)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Collection plan]
An arrow key is a pair $(\tau,\sigma)$ representing $\tau\to\sigma$.  The total
collector recursively gathers all lambda occurrences and every arrow node in
the source signature and term, removing duplicate arrow keys.  Its result is
\[
  P(e)=(\mathsf{arrows}(e),\mathsf{closures}(e)).
\]
The collector is a total Lean function, and bounds and uniqueness lemmas show
that every capture position names an actual context entry.
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/Collect.lean}
\end{definition}

The executable content of collection can be summarized independently of its
Lean representation as follows.  Here $\mathsf{dedup}$ preserves first-occurrence
order, and $\mathbin{+\!+}$ denotes list concatenation.

\begin{pseudocode}{$\mathsf{collect}(\Sigma,e)$}
$\alpha \gets \mathsf{dedup}(\mathsf{arrowsInSignature}(\Sigma)
  \mathbin{+\!+}\mathsf{arrowsInTerm}(e))$ \\
$\kappa \gets \mathsf{closures}(e)$ \\
\textbf{return} $(\alpha,\kappa)$ \\
\\[-1mm]
\textbf{procedure} $\mathsf{closures}(e)$ \\
\>\textbf{match} $e$ \textbf{with} \\
\>\>$x_i, c_k, b$ $\Rightarrow []$ \\
\>\>$\lambda x{:}\tau.e'$ $\Rightarrow
  [(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e')]\mathbin{+\!+}\mathsf{closures}(e')$ \\
\>\>$e_1\,e_2$ $\Rightarrow
  \mathsf{closures}(e_1)\mathbin{+\!+}\mathsf{closures}(e_2)$ \\
\>\>$\circ(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ $\Rightarrow
  \mathsf{concat}(\mathsf{map}(\mathsf{closures},[e_1,\ldots,e_n]))$ \\
\>\textbf{end match}
\end{pseudocode}

For the classic proof core, each arrow key generates a unary symbol
\[
 \app_{\tau,\sigma}:
 \mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma)\times\erase{\tau}\longrightarrow\erase{\sigma},
\]
and each $\chi=(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e)$ generates
\[
 \clos_\chi:
 \erase{\gamma_{i_1}}\times\cdots\times\erase{\gamma_{i_m}}
 \longrightarrow\mathsf{Fn}(\tau,\sigma),
\]
where $i_1,\ldots,i_m$ are the exact captures of $e$.

\section{Core defunctionalization}
\sectioncontents{Translation on syntax; closure equations; complete target theory.}

\begin{definition}[Core translation]
The total, type-preserving translation satisfies
\[
 \Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e:\tau
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \erase{\Sigma};\erase{\Gamma}^{\star}\vdash\df{e}:\erase{\tau}.
\]
Variables, constants, Boolean constructs, equality, and quantifiers translate
homomorphically.  Its essential clauses are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \df{\lambda x{:}\tau.e}
   &=\clos_\chi(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_m}),\\
 \df{f\,a}&=\app_{\tau,\sigma}(\df{f},\df{a}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\chi=(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e)$ and $i_1,\ldots,i_m$ are its captures.
Source constants are nullary target symbols in this classic core.
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/Core.lean}
\end{definition}

The complete syntax-directed procedure is shown below.  The notation
$\mathsf{mapChildren}$ means recursive, constructor-preserving traversal of the
Boolean, equality, and quantifier forms.

\begin{pseudocode}{$\df{e}$}
\textbf{match} $e$ \textbf{with} \\
\>$x_i$ $\Rightarrow x_{\erase{i}}$ \\
\>$c_k$ $\Rightarrow c_k^{\mathsf{FO}}()$ \\
\>$b$ $\Rightarrow b$ \\
\>$\lambda x{:}\tau.e'$ $\Rightarrow$ \\
\>\>$\chi\gets(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e')$ \\
\>\>$(i_1,\ldots,i_m)\gets\mathsf{captureRefs}(\chi)$ \\
\>\>\textbf{return} $\clos_\chi(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_m})$ \\
\>$e_1\,e_2$ $\Rightarrow
  \app_{\tau,\sigma}(\df{e_1},\df{e_2})$ \\
\>$\circ(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ $\Rightarrow
  \circ(\df{e_1},\ldots,\df{e_n})$ \\
\textbf{end match}
\end{pseudocode}

In the final clause, $\circ$ ranges only over constructors retained by the
target: Boolean literals and connectives, equality, and quantifiers.  Thus the
procedure is total and has no ``unsupported syntax'' case for the core language.

\begin{definition}[Closure equation]
For $\chi=(\Gamma,\tau,\sigma,e)$, let $\bar x$ enumerate all of $\Gamma$ and
let $x$ denote the lambda argument.  The defining equation is
\[
 \forall\bar x\,x.\quad
 \app_{\tau,\sigma}
   (\clos_\chi(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_m}),x)=\df{e}.
\]
Only captured variables are constructor arguments, although all variables from
the original context are quantified.  Let $\mathcal E(e)$ be the list of these
equations for every lambda occurrence in $e$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Defunctionalization theory]
For a source sentence $\varphi$,
\[
  \mathcal T(\varphi)=\mathcal E(\varphi)\mathbin{+\!+}[\df{\varphi}].
\]
Thus a target model of $\mathcal T(\varphi)$ must satisfy both all closure
equations and the translated query.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/ModelExtension.lean}
\end{definition}

\section{The source--target logical relation}
\sectioncontents{Relation at each type; related valuations; compatibility of models.}

Fix a source model $\mathcal M_s$, a target model $\mathcal M_t$, and relations
$r_\beta\subseteq B(\beta)\times C_B(\beta)$ for opaque base sorts.

\begin{definition}[Value relation]
The relation $\vrel{\nu_s}{R,\tau}{\nu_t}$ is defined by recursion on $\tau$:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
 \vrel{p}{R,\Bool}{q} &\Longleftrightarrow& p\Leftrightarrow q,\\
 \vrel{a}{R,\Base(\beta)}{b} &\Longleftrightarrow& r_\beta(a,b),\\
 \vrel{f}{R,\tau\to\sigma}{g} &\Longleftrightarrow&
   \forall a,b.\;\vrel{a}{R,\tau}{b}\Rightarrow
   \vrel{f(a)}{R,\sigma}{\app_{\tau,\sigma}^{\mathcal M_t}(g,b)}.
\end{array}
\]
The arrow clause is the semantic heart of defunctionalization: the opaque target
value $g$ behaves like $f$ whenever it is observed through the generated
application symbol.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/LogicalRelation.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Related valuations]
$\rrel{\rho_s}{R}{\rho_t}$ holds when every $i:\tau\in\Gamma$ satisfies
\[
  \vrel{\rho_s(i)}{R,\tau}{\rho_t(\erase{i})}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Extension]
If $\rrel{\rho_s}{R}{\rho_t}$ and $\vrel{\nu_s}{R,\tau}{\nu_t}$, then
$\rrel{\rho_s[\nu_s]}{R}{\rho_t[\nu_t]}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Case analysis on the typed de Bruijn reference: the head uses the new hypothesis,
and a tail reference uses the old valuation relation.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Related models]
A model relation $R:\mathcal M_s\sim\mathcal M_t$ packages the base relations
and five compatibility obligations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item corresponding source and target constants are related;
 \item equality of related source values is equivalent to equality of their
       target values;
 \item the value relation is left-total;
 \item the value relation is right-total;
 \item every translated closure constructor is related to the denotation of its
       source lambda under related valuations.
\end{enumerate}
The two totality conditions are exactly what allow quantifiers in either model
to be transported to the other.
\end{definition}

\section{Semantic preservation}
\sectioncontents{Fundamental lemma; sentence equivalence; canonical model extension; unsatisfiability soundness.}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental lemma]
Suppose $R:\mathcal M_s\sim\mathcal M_t$, $\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e:\tau$, and
$\rrel{\rho_s}{R}{\rho_t}$.  Then
\[
  \vrel{\den{e}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}}{R,\tau}
       {\den{\df{e}}{\mathcal M_t}{\rho_t}}.
\]
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/Fundamental.lean}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof structure]
By induction on $e$.  Variables use related valuations and constants use model
compatibility.  Boolean cases use propositional congruence.  Equality uses
equality compatibility.  Lambda uses the closure obligation, and application
unfolds the arrow clause of the logical relation.  For $\forall$, right-totality
turns an arbitrary target value into a related source value in one direction,
and left-totality does the converse; $\exists$ uses the dual witness argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed-formula preservation]
For a sentence $\varphi$ and related models,
\[
  \mathcal M_s\sat\varphi
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \mathcal M_t\sat\df{\varphi}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Empty valuations are related vacuously.  Instantiate the fundamental lemma at
$\Bool$, where the logical relation is logical equivalence.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Canonical target model}

Given $\mathcal M_s$, the canonical model interprets target base sorts by source
base carriers and target function sorts by a canonical representation of source
functions.  Its application symbols apply represented functions, source symbols
use source interpretations, and closure constructors install their exact
captured environment.  The capture-agreement development proves that changing
uncaptured entries cannot affect the body denotation.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/ModelExtension.lean}

\begin{theorem}[Closure validity]
For every source model $\mathcal M_s$ and term $e$, its canonical target model
$\widehat{\mathcal M_s}$ satisfies all collected closure equations:
\[
  \widehat{\mathcal M_s}\satt\mathcal E(e).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Model extension]
If $\mathcal M_s\sat\varphi$, then
\[
  \widehat{\mathcal M_s}\satt\mathcal T(\varphi).
\]
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/ModelExtension.lean}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Closure validity handles $\mathcal E(\varphi)$.  The canonical models are
related, so closed-formula preservation transports the truth of $\varphi$ to
$\df{\varphi}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Target unsatisfiability implies source unsatisfiability]
For every closed source formula $\varphi$,
\[
 \operatorname{Unsat}_{\mathrm{FO}}(\mathcal T(\varphi))
 \Longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Unsat}_{\mathrm{HO}}(\varphi).
\]
\leanfile{Defunctionalization/ModelExtension.lean}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume a source model satisfies $\varphi$.  Model extension constructs a target
model satisfying $\mathcal T(\varphi)$, contradicting target unsatisfiability.
\end{proof}

\section{Eta expansion and flattened application}
\sectioncontents{Eta-long form; semantic eta correctness; production-style flattened symbols.}

The production encoding uses one fully flattened symbol for a complete arrow
spine.  If
\[
 \tau=\tau_1\to\cdots\to\tau_n\to\sigma
\]
with non-arrow result $\sigma$, its declaration has the shape
\[
 \app_\tau:
 \mathsf{Fn}(\tau_1,\tau_2\to\cdots\to\sigma)
 \times\erase{\tau_1}\times\cdots\times\erase{\tau_n}
 \longrightarrow\erase{\sigma}.
\]
An under-applied symbol cannot represent a residual function, so partial
applications must be converted to closures.

\begin{definition}[Eta-long normalization]
$\eta(e)$ recursively normalizes subterms.  Whenever the reconstructed term has
arrow type $\tau\to\sigma$ and is not already a lambda, it becomes
\[
  \lambda x{:}\tau.\eta(e\,x),
\]
recursing through $\sigma$ as needed.  Existing lambdas retain their outer
binder.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/Eta.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{pseudocode}{$\eta(e)$}
$e'\gets\mathsf{mapChildren}(\eta,e)$ \\
\textbf{if} $\mathsf{type}(e')=\tau\to\sigma$ \textbf{then} \\
\>\textbf{match} $e'$ \textbf{with} \\
\>\>$\lambda x{:}\tau.e''$ $\Rightarrow
  \lambda x{:}\tau.e''$ \\
\>\>$\_$ $\Rightarrow
  \lambda x{:}\tau.\eta_{\sigma}(\mathsf{weaken}(e')\,x)$ \\
\>\textbf{end match} \\
\textbf{else return} $e'$
\end{pseudocode}

\begin{lemma}[Arrow-value invariant]
If $\Sigma;\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\to\sigma$, then $\eta(e)$ is syntactically a
lambda.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Eta semantic preservation]
For every $\mathcal M_s$, $\rho_s$, and $e$,
\[
  \den{\eta(e)}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}
  =\den{e}{\mathcal M_s}{\rho_s}.
\]
In particular, eta-expanding a partial application preserves its residual
function value exactly.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/EtaCorrectness.lean}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Flattened application preservation]
Under the canonical interpretation of a production application symbol, feeding
the translated argument spine to the single flattened symbol yields the same
result as iterated source application.\leanfile{Defunctionalization/FlattenedApplication.lean}
\end{theorem}

Together, eta correctness and flattened-application preservation justify the
production representation at the typed semantic layer.  They are separate from
the classic unary-core fundamental theorem; connecting concrete emitted syntax
to these objects is a refinement obligation.

\section{Guarded subtype encodings}
\sectioncontents{Encoding contract; guarded quantifiers and equality; natural numbers.}

\begin{definition}[Guarded encoding]
A guarded encoding from $S$ to $T$ consists of nonempty $S$, a map
$\epsilon:S\to T$, a predicate $G:T\to\mathsf{Prop}$, and a decoder
$\delta:(t:T)\to G(t)\to S$ such that
\[
 G(\epsilon(s)),\qquad
 \delta(\epsilon(s))=s,\qquad
 G(t)\Rightarrow\epsilon(\delta(t))=t.
\]
Thus $S$ is equivalent to the guarded subtype $\{t:T\mid G(t)\}$.
\leanfile{Guarded/Encoding.lean}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Guarded quantifiers]
If predicates $P:S\to\mathsf{Prop}$ and $Q:T\to\mathsf{Prop}$ agree on encoded
values, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (\forall s, P(s))&\Longleftrightarrow
   \forall t, G(t)\Rightarrow Q(t),\\
 (\exists s, P(s))&\Longleftrightarrow
   \exists t, G(t)\land Q(t).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover $\epsilon(s_1)=\epsilon(s_2)$ iff $s_1=s_2$.
\end{lemma}

The concrete natural-number encoding takes $S=\mathbb N$, $T=\mathbb Z$,
$\epsilon(n)=n$, and $G(z)\equiv 0\le z$.  The Lean development also lifts
functions while enforcing guarded results and supports optional guarded fields.

\section{Certified refinement to the live translator}
\sectioncontents{Typed bridges; capture and command certificates; extension hooks; trusted boundary.}

The semantic theorem above speaks about proof-facing syntax.  Production Crush
starts with Lean expressions and emits named SMT commands.  The bridge modules
attach typed witnesses to the parts of that process covered by the core:
\begin{itemize}
 \item type witnesses relate normalized, nondependent Lean arrows to source types
       and generated first-order declarations;\leanfile{Bridge/Type.lean}
 \item term and context bridges reify supported Lean expressions into intrinsic
       terms;\leanfiles{Bridge/Term.lean and Bridge/Reify.lean}
 \item capture certificates connect the live free-variable collector to exact,
       duplicate-free typed closure captures;\leanfile{Bridge/Capture.lean}
 \item command certificates retain the declarations and guarded equations that
       were actually emitted.\leanfile{Bridge/Command.lean}
\end{itemize}

Certified hooks state semantic preservation for restricted primitive mappings.
The built-in Boolean negation exercises this path.  Arbitrary metaprogram
handlers remain an explicit trusted boundary rather than receiving a vacuous
certificate.\leanfile{Hooks.lean}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the theorem]
The complete theorem is established for every constructor of the modeled core.
The live translator routes the supported fragment through proof-carrying bridge
objects and falls back explicitly outside it.  Therefore one should distinguish
(i) the fully proved core semantic theorem, (ii) proved eta, flattening, guarded
encoding, and hook components, and (iii) the remaining refinement assumptions
for unsupported Lean expressions, recursive datatype well-formedness discovery,
and unrestricted trusted handlers.
\end{remark}

 \item translated values of every source term are logically related;
 \item closed source and translated formulas agree in related models;
 \item the canonical model satisfies every generated closure equation;
 \item every satisfying source model extends to a target model;

 \item guarded universal, existential, equality, and result encodings are sound.
\end{enumerate}
